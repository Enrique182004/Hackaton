# Acanthamoeba isolation from lake water: sterile sampling and workflow

Free-living amoebae (FLA) such as *Acanthamoeba* are isolated from environmental water by culture on non-nutrient agar seeded with bacteria, then confirmed by morphology and molecular methods.

## Biosafety
Work at BSL-2 practice. *Acanthamoeba* can cause Acanthamoeba keratitis and granulomatous amoebic encephalitis, so handle cultures in a biosafety cabinet where possible. Wear PPE (lab coat, nitrile gloves, eye protection). Disinfect benches before and after work. Autoclave all contaminated plates, tubes and consumables (121 °C, at least 15-20 min) before disposal. Flame-sterilize loops and spreaders, and sterilize forceps by flame or autoclave between uses.

## 1. Sample collection
- Use sterile, pre-autoclaved or pre-rinsed bottles (for example 500 mL to 1 L).
- Wear gloves; collect from just below the surface, away from the shore edge; open the bottle only at the moment of sampling and avoid touching the inside of the cap.
- Record site, GPS, date, time, water temperature, pH and turbidity; also note the nearby soil type and runoff conditions.
- Keep samples cool (not frozen) and process within about 24 hours; cold can encourage encystment, so avoid prolonged refrigeration if possible.

## 2. Concentration by filtration
- Filter a measured volume (for example 100-500 mL) through a sterile cellulose nitrate or mixed-ester membrane (about 0.45 to 1.2 µm pore) using a sterilized filtration unit.
- Place the membrane face down on the agar, or resuspend the retained material in sterile Page's amoeba saline (PAS) and plate the suspension.

## 3. Culture on non-nutrient agar (NNA) with E. coli
- Prepare 1.5% non-nutrient agar in PAS or Page's saline; autoclave and pour plates.
- Prepare a lawn of heat-killed *E. coli* (for example a suspension heat-treated at about 60-65 °C or autoclaved) over the agar surface as food. Heat-killing prevents bacterial overgrowth.
- Inoculate the plate with the filter or sample, seal with parafilm or gas-permeable tape.
- Incubate at about 30 °C (range 25-37 °C; higher temperatures can select thermotolerant, potentially pathogenic isolates).

## 4. Observation
- Examine daily for up to 2 weeks with an inverted microscope (10x-40x).
- Trophozoites move slowly with spine-like projections (acanthopodia) and a contractile vacuole; they leave grazing tracks in the bacterial lawn.
- Double-walled cysts (a wrinkled ectocyst and polygonal or stellate endocyst) form as food runs out.
- Classify morphologically using Page's key (for example Pussard and Pons groups I-III for *Acanthamoeba* based on cyst size and shape).

## 5. Purification
- Cut a small agar block from the leading edge of growth and transfer it, upside down, to a fresh NNA/E. coli plate; repeat until the culture is free of fungi and other protists. Optional axenization uses PYG medium.

## 6. Molecular confirmation
- Extract DNA from trophozoites or cysts (bead beating or proteinase K plus a commercial kit).
- PCR amplify the 18S rDNA gene (for example the genus-specific primers JDP1 and JDP2, which amplify the ASA.S1 region of about 450-500 bp); include positive and negative controls.
- Sequence and compare with GenBank (BLAST) to assign the genotype (T1-T23; T4 is the most common clinical genotype).

## Common sterile-technique mistakes to check on a flowchart
- No sterilization of bottles, membranes or media (autoclave) before use.
- No flame sterilization of loops or forceps between transfers.
- Using live rather than heat-killed bacteria for the lawn.
- Missing negative control plates or PCR controls.
- No autoclaving of waste before disposal.
