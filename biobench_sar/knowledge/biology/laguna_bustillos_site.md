# Laguna de Bustillos and surrounding soils (Cuauhtémoc, Chihuahua)

Note: this is a general background summary for planning, not a measured dataset. Verify local values in the field.

## Site overview
- Laguna de Bustillos is a shallow, endorheic (closed-basin) lake near Cuauhtémoc, Chihuahua, in the highlands of northern Mexico. Water arrives by surface runoff and streams and leaves mainly by evaporation, so dissolved salts and nutrients tend to concentrate.
- Water level fluctuates strongly with the rainy season (roughly summer) and drought; shallow depth means wide temperature swings and frequent resuspension of sediment.
- The basin has intensive agriculture (such as apples, maize and beans), livestock and urban areas, so runoff may carry sediment, nutrients, organic matter and agrochemical residues. Such conditions can favour bacterial growth, which in turn supports bacterivorous amoebae.

## Soil classes in the area
### Leptosol
- Shallow, often stony or gravelly soil over hard rock; low water-holding capacity and low organic matter.
- Tends to produce fast surface runoff and is prone to erosion on slopes, so runoff may carry mineral sediment and raise turbidity (suspended solids) quickly after storms, with comparatively fewer dissolved nutrients.
- Higher turbidity can reduce light and make microscopy and filtration harder (membranes clog sooner; larger volumes of sample may be needed).

### Feozem (Phaeozem)
- Dark, humus-rich, fertile and deeper soil, typically with a good structure; widely used for crops and pasture.
- Better infiltration and water retention, but when runoff occurs it can carry organic matter, nitrogen and phosphorus, and fertilizer residues, which tends to promote microbial growth (eutrophication) rather than just mineral turbidity.
- Organic-rich, bacteria-rich water is plausibly more favourable to free-living amoebae such as *Acanthamoeba*, though this should be tested rather than assumed.

## Implications for sampling and amoeba ecology
- Acanthamoeba is ubiquitous in soil and freshwater, tolerates drying (cysts), temperature swings and a wide pH range; presence is expected, so the aim is to compare sites.
- Choose sites that contrast the two soil types: for example a shore receiving runoff from Leptosol slopes versus one receiving Feozem agricultural runoff, plus an open-water reference. Sample before and after rain if possible.
- Record turbidity, temperature, pH, conductivity and nearby land use at each site; collect sediment and water.
- Expect clogging with turbid samples; filter smaller volumes in replicate, and consider pre-settling or coarse pre-filtration.
- Replicate every site (at least triplicate) and always take the same volume for comparability.
